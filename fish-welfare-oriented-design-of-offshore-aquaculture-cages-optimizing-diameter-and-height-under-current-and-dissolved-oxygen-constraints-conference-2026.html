Fish welfare–oriented design of offshore aquaculture cages: Optimizing diameter and height under current and dissolved oxygen constraints

Xueliang Wen, Muk Chen Ong, Rune Schlanbusch

Tokyo, Japan · June 7–12, 2026

Abstract

Global warming represents the most profound aspect of ongoing climate change. The year 2024 was the warmest on record since 1850. Global mean temperature exceeded 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels for the second time—the first being in 2023—surpassing the 1.5°C threshold set as a key target in the Paris Agreement. Under the pressure of global warming, nearshore aquaculture environments in Norway face increasing challenges, including warmer waters and their adverse impacts on fish farming, such as sea lice infestations, harmful algal blooms, and jellyfish outbreaks. These challenges have accelerated the shift toward offshore aquaculture, where deeper and colder waters, combined with improved water exchange, provide a more favourable environment for sustainable fish production. However, offshore aquaculture also faces significant fish welfare challenges, including stress and fatigue under high waves and strong currents, as well as limited oxygen supply in surface waters. These factors can lead to reduced appetite, poor feed conversion, stunted growth, and even acute mortality. Therefore, offshore cages must be carefully designed to mitigate these risks and safeguard fish welfare. This study investigates the cage size effects of semi-submersible cages based on the fish welfare related to high- and low-current conditions. The high- and low-current conditions and ambient dissolved oxygen (DO) were taken from measured metocean data at a proposed site Norskerenna Sør. A theoretical model is developed to predict the distribution of DO levels at different water depths inside sea cages under low-current conditions. This model is derived from the transport equation of the DO, where the fish is assumed to be uniformly distributed and the flow velocity is assumed to be constant but slightly smaller than the ambient current speed. The flow velocity distributions within the cages under high-current conditions are calculated using an empirical wake model. Based on the analyses of low- and high-current limits, feasible ranges for cage diameter and height are established. These results delineate a feasible design range of the fish cage diameter and height and provide valuable insights for cage geometry and biomass management across realistic environmental conditions.
