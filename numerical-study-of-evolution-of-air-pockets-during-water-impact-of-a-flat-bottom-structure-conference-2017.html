Numerical study of evolution of air pockets during water impact of a flat-bottom structure

Qiulin Qu, Gaohe Ji, Peiqing Liu, Xueliang Wen, Ramesh K. Agarwal

Grapevine, Texas, USA · January 9–13, 2017

Abstract

The evolution of air pockets during the water impact of a rigid two-dimensional flat-bottom structure is investigated by solving the Reynolds-Averaged Navier-Stokes equations in conjunction with the realizable k-ε turbulence model. The free water surface is captured by the Volume of Fluid method. In the simulation, both the air and water are considered as compressible fluids, and the viscosity, gravity and surface tension effects are taken into consideration. The impact pressure shows oscillatory characteristics during the water impact. The formation, shift and breakup of air pockets and the flow physics behind them is investigated. The relationship between the air pockets and the oscillations in the impact pressure is obtained for the first time in the literature. In the air pockets forming process, the air in the cushion is compressed by the bottom and therefore the impact pressure increases; in the air pockets breakup process, the compressed air releases and thus the impact pressure decreases.
