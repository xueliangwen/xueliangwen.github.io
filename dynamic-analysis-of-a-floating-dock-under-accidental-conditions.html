Dynamic analysis of a floating dock under accidental conditions

Xueliang Wen, Alejandro García Conde, Jianan Zhang, Muk Chen Ong

2024

Abstract

The utilization of floating docks has an inherently higher risk than their ground counterpart and can result in severe accidents due to the possibility of stability loss and structure failure. To minimize the occurrence of accidents, it is essential to conduct comprehensive testing on the dock's behaviours and identify potential points of failure that could lead to severe accidents using an efficient numerical method for assessing the dynamic response of a floating dock under accidental conditions. The numerical method incorporates a a six-degree-of-freedom (6-DOF) model, a hydrostatic force model, a hydrodynamic force model, a hydraulic model, and a modified P-controller. The model's sensitivity of section number, point number on a section, and time step is assessed through a sensitivity study. A dynamic analysis of the investigated floating dock during the ballasting and de-ballasting operations with valve and pump failures is conducted. The present numerical results show that the modified P-controller can handle all one-valve failures, most of two-valve failures and half of the pump failures during the de-ballasting operations, as well as a few cases of valve failures during the ballasting operations. A practical remedial measure of disabling a symmetrical pump is proposed to prevent the dock-vessel system from entering hazardous conditions. The measure proves effective for all severe two-valve failure and the one-pump failure scenarios during the de-ballasting operations. Overall, the present model can improve the safety and stability of the floating dock system by considering potential failure scenarios and proposing appropriate remedial measures.
